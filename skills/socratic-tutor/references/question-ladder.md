# The question ladder

Six rungs. Start at the lowest rung the student has not already cleared, and move up one rung per correct answer. Move down when an answer shows a gap.

| Rung | Purpose | Example question (solving 2x^2 - 5x - 3 = 0) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Clarify | Make sure they have read the problem | "What is the question asking you to find, in your own words?" |
| 2. Recall | Surface the idea they need | "What methods do you know for solving a quadratic?" |
| 3. Simpler case | Try the idea where it is easy | "Could you factor x^2 - 5x + 6 first? What two numbers do you need?" |
| 4. Apply | Use it on the actual problem | "Now the leading coefficient is 2. What pairs of factors could the first terms come from?" |
| 5. Check | Verify the answer | "Put x = 3 back into the original equation. What do you get?" |
| 6. Generalise | Name what transfers | "When would you choose the formula over factoring?" |

The same ladder on a physics problem (a 2 kg block pushed with 6 N across a floor with 2 N of friction):

| Rung | Example question |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Clarify | "What are you asked to find, and what is given, with units?" |
| 2. Recall | "Which law connects force, mass and acceleration?" |
| 3. Simpler case | "If there were no friction, what would the acceleration be?" |
| 4. Apply | "Friction acts against the push. What is the net force now?" |
| 5. Check | "Does the sign of your answer match the direction the block moves?" |
| 6. Generalise | "What changes in your method if the floor is a slope?" |

## Hint escalation on one rung

Use when the student stalls on the same rung twice.

1. **Nudge.** Point at the relevant part. "Look at the constant term. What are its factor pairs?"
2. **Name the concept.** "This is the product-sum method with a leading coefficient. The product you need is 2 times -3."
3. **Worked partial step.** "The product is -6 and the sum is -5, so the pair is -6 and +1. Split the middle term: 2x^2 - 6x + x - 3. Can you finish?"
4. **Full solution.** Give it in full, then ask one question that checks the key step: "Why did we need the product to be -6 rather than -3?"

## Signs you are on the wrong rung

- The student answers instantly and correctly: go up two rungs.
- The student answers with a guess or a question: go down one rung.
- The student repeats the same wrong step: switch from questions to a plain statement of the error, then return to questions.
- The student answers a different question from the one you asked: ask it again in fewer words.

## Length of a session

A homework problem takes four to eight exchanges. If the ladder has not reached rung 5 after ten exchanges, the blocker is a concept from earlier in the course; name it, give a two-sentence explanation, and return to the problem.
