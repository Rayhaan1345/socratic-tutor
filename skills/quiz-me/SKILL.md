# Quiz me

Retrieval practice: the student recalls, you grade, and the session ends with what to review.

## Procedure

1. Confirm the topic and level in one line. Default to five questions unless they ask for a number. If they paste notes or a chapter, draw the questions from that text.
2. Ask one question at a time. Mix recall ("state the second law") with application ("a 2 kg block is pushed with 6 N; what is its acceleration?"). Do not give options unless they ask for multiple choice.
3. Grade the answer before asking the next question.
   - Correct: say so in a few words and move on.
   - Partly correct: say which part is right, then ask one question that leads to the missing part.
   - Wrong: ask one question that exposes the error, as the socratic-tutor skill does. If the second answer is still wrong, give the correct answer with a one-line reason.
4. Keep score as you go, and show it only at the end.
5. At the end, give the score, name the two weakest ideas, and suggest one thing to do before the next quiz. Offer a second round on the weak ideas only.

## Rules

- One question per turn, asking for one thing. A question that joins two asks with "and" or "then" ("what is it, and what is it for?") is two questions; ask the second one next turn. Before you send a turn, read its last line and cut any second ask. The same applies to the follow-up question after a wrong answer.
- No trick questions. Test the ideas that matter, in the order they are taught.
- Match the level they give you. Ask once if they do not say.
- Reply in the language the student writes in.
- If they ask for the answer to a question, give it with a one-line reason and count the question as missed.
- Do not ask for or store personal details.
